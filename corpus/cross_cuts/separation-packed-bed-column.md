## Adsorption column chromatography (Tsvet) (1906)

- **id**: `tswett-1906-adsorption-chromatography`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Mikhail Tsvet (Mikhail Tswett) (University of Warsaw)
- **disclosure**: Tswett, M. Physikalisch-chemische Studien uber das Chlorophyll. Die Adsorptionen. Ber. Dtsch. Bot. Ges. 1906, 24, 316-323
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: The original chromatographic separation: differential adsorption of analytes onto a packed solid as a mobile solvent flows through, resolving a mixture into spatially distinct bands. Anticipates the operating principle of every packed-bed separation column, including microfluidic on-chip chromatography and solid-phase extraction beds used for sample cleanup in lab-on-chip cartridges. The conceptual foundation of all column separation; cannot be claimed as novel by any later chromatographic device.

## Partition chromatography (1941)

- **id**: `martin-synge-1941-partition-chromatography`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Archer Martin, Richard Synge (Wool Industries Research Association, Leeds)
- **disclosure**: Martin, A. J. P.; Synge, R. L. M. A new form of chromatogram employing two liquid phases. Biochem. J. 1941, 35, 1358-1368. DOI: 10.1042/bj0351358
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: Introduced partition (rather than adsorption) as the basis of chromatographic separation and the theoretical-plate model of column performance. Anticipates the operating principle of every microfluidic chromatography column, on-chip LC, and packed-bed separation device, plus the plate-height analysis used to characterize them. The 1941 paper also explicitly proposed gas as the mobile phase, anticipating gas chromatography. Microfluidic separation columns claiming novelty in partition-based retention contend with this disclosure.
