## Mullis-Saiki 1985 Cetus PCR Foundational Disclosure (1985-12-20)

- **id**: `mullis-cetus-pcr-1985-foundational-anchor`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Kary Mullis, Randall Saiki et al. (Cetus Corporation)
- **disclosure**: Saiki, Scharf, Faloona, Mullis, Horn, Erlich, Arnheim 'Enzymatic amplification of beta-globin genomic sequences and restriction site analysis for diagnosis of sickle cell anemia' Science 230:1350-1354 (1985-12-20) doi:10.1126/science.2999980; US4683202 'Process for amplifying nucleic acid sequences' (priority 1985-10-25, issued 1987)
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: The 1985 Science paper and US4683202 are the foundational disclosures of the polymerase chain reaction. Now expired (US4683202 lapsed 2005, US4683195 lapsed 2005, US4965188 lapsed 2007), placing the core PCR method in the public domain in all jurisdictions. Material to defensive publication because: (a) any post-expiration claim attempting to recapture core PCR (cyclic denaturation/annealing/extension with thermostable polymerase) by narrow re-framing is anticipated; (b) the Cetus disclosure provides explicit support for using Klenow fragment, Taq polymerase, primer-directed amplification, and gel-based product detection; (c) all downstream PCR-cartridge patents must distinguish over this art for any claim element they cannot anchor to a post-1985 inventive contribution. Useful as the universal anchor in any modern molecular-diagnostic patent invalidity analysis.

## Fodor 1991 Affymax/Affymetrix Light-Directed Microarray Patent (1991-02-15)

- **id**: `fodor-1991-affymax-microarray-patent`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: lab-on-chip
- **creator**: Stephen P. A. Fodor et al. (Affymax / Affymetrix)
- **disclosure**: Fodor et al. 'Light-directed, spatially addressable parallel chemical synthesis' Science 251:767-773 (1991-02-15) doi:10.1126/science.1990438; US5143854 'Light-directed, spatially addressable parallel chemical synthesis' (priority 1989, issued 1992)
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Foundational 1991 patent for light-directed combinatorial in-situ chemical synthesis on solid supports, in which photolabile protecting groups on growing oligo chains are selectively removed by patterned UV illumination through a photomask, enabling parallel synthesis of millions of unique sequences in a single chip. Anticipates: (a) any in-situ-synthesized DNA microarray architecture using mask- or DMD-based photolithography; (b) the architectural pattern of using semiconductor lithography tools to manufacture biological probe arrays; (c) photolabile NPPOC/MeNPOC protecting group chemistries adapted for nucleic-acid synthesis. The Affymetrix GeneChip product line and downstream Roche NimbleGen, Agilent SurePrint, and Twist silicon-DRIE arrays trace back to this disclosure. Patent expired 2010.

## Sano 1992 ImmunoPCR Foundational Disclosure (1992-10-02)

- **id**: `sano-1992-immunopcr-foundational-anchor`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Takeshi Sano, Cassandra L. Smith, Charles R. Cantor (Boston University / Univ. Heidelberg)
- **disclosure**: Sano, Smith & Cantor 'Immuno-PCR: very sensitive antigen detection by means of specific antibody-DNA conjugates' Science 258:120-122 (1992-10-02) doi:10.1126/science.1439758
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: The 1992 Science paper is the foundational disclosure of immuno-PCR: an antibody is conjugated to a DNA reporter sequence; antigen capture is followed by PCR amplification of the reporter, providing 10^5-10^7-fold sensitivity gain over conventional ELISA. Material to defensive publication because: (a) all subsequent oligo-conjugated-antibody readout chemistries (CITE-seq, AbSeq, Tapestri Multi-omics, proximity-extension assays) trace lineage to this disclosure; (b) the paper's 1992 publication date establishes 102 prior art against any later patent attempting to claim antibody-oligo PCR readout broadly; (c) the academic origin (Sano was at Boston University; Cantor was at Boston Univ. and Univ. Heidelberg affiliations) places the technology in the public domain absent a downstream university filing. The work is incorporated by reference in nearly all single-cell multi-omics patent literature.

## Chiou & Wu 2005 Optoelectronic Tweezers Academic Anchor (Berkeley UCB) (2005-07-21)

- **id**: `chiou-wu-2005-oet-academic-anchor`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: single-cell-platform
- **creator**: Pei Yu Chiou, Aaron T. Ohta, Ming C. Wu (UC Berkeley)
- **disclosure**: Chiou, Ohta & Wu, 'Massively parallel manipulation of single cells and microparticles using optical images', Nature 436:370-372 (2005-07-21) doi:10.1038/nature03831; UC Berkeley provisional filings 2003-2004
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Foundational 2005 academic disclosure of optoelectronic tweezers (OET): an amorphous-silicon photoconductor sandwiched between transparent electrodes is illuminated by patterned light to create position-controllable DEP traps. Cells and beads are manipulated by re-shaping the light pattern in real time. This Nature paper is the 102 prior-art anchor for any post-2005 patent claim on light-defined DEP particle manipulation, and is incorporated by reference in Berkeley Lights' downstream patent estate. Confirms 2005 public availability of: (i) photoconductive sandwich substrates, (ii) DMD/projector-driven DEP control, (iii) parallel single-cell manipulation, (iv) operation at electrically-safe MHz frequencies.
