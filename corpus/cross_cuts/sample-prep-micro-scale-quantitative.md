## Microchemical analysis (Behrens crystal-test method) (1894)

- **id**: `behrens-1894-microchemical-analysis`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Heinrich Behrens (Delft Polytechnic / Technische Hogeschool Delft)
- **disclosure**: Behrens, H. Anleitung zur mikrochemischen Analyse. Voss, Hamburg/Leipzig, 1894-1897
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: One of the earliest systematic treatments of qualitative chemical analysis performed in microliter droplets observed under the microscope (crystal-form identification of cations and anions). Background prior art for the very idea of a microscope-slide-scale analytical device. Cited to establish that slide-scale chemical identification predates modern microfluidics by a century.

## Quantitative microchemical analysis (Emich method) (1900)

- **id**: `emich-1900-microchemistry`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Friedrich Emich (Graz University of Technology)
- **disclosure**: Emich, F. Lehrbuch der Mikrochemie. Wiesbaden, 1911 (methods from circa 1900); see Emich, F. Methoden der Mikrochemie. Z. Anal. Chem. 1910, 49, 1-25
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: An early systematization of chemistry done at the microliter and sub-microliter scale, including purpose-built micro-apparatus (capillaries, micro-vessels, microscope-slide reaction spots). Conceptual ancestor of spot-test microfluidics and droplet-scale chemistry. Cited as background prior art establishing that handling and reacting microliter volumes in dedicated small-scale glassware is a discipline more than a century old; relevant to claims framing droplet-scale or slide-scale chemical analysis as fundamentally new.

## Quantitative organic microanalysis (1911)

- **id**: `pregl-1911-quantitative-organic-microanalysis`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Fritz Pregl (University of Graz / University of Innsbruck)
- **disclosure**: Pregl, F. Die quantitative organische Mikroanalyse. Springer, Berlin, 1917 (methods developed and presented from 1911; Nobel Prize 1923)
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: The methodological root of microscale quantitative analysis. By cutting sample requirements by roughly two orders of magnitude and developing the microbalance and micro-combustion apparatus, Pregl established the premise that underlies all of microfluidics: that complete quantitative analysis can be performed on vanishingly small samples. While not microfluidic in the channel sense, it is the conceptual ancestor cited when arguing that miniaturized total analysis is an incremental scaling of a century-old program rather than a novel paradigm. Relevant background prior art for claims framing micro-volume quantitative analysis as inherently novel.
