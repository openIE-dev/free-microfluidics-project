## Heinzl / Hertz 1979 Lund Binary Continuous Jet System (1979)

- **id**: `heinzl-hertz-1979-binary-continuous-jet-lund`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: inkjet-printhead
- **creator**: Joachim Heinzl (Munich), Carl Hellmuth Hertz (Lund University)
- **disclosure**: Joachim Heinzl and Carl Hellmuth Hertz, 'Ink-Jet Printing', Advances in Electronics and Electron Physics, vol 65, pp 91-171 (1985); earlier Lund binary continuous-jet disclosures from 1977-1979 era; Hertz US patents 4,346,387 and related
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Academic and patent anchor for the Hertz binary continuous-inkjet variant developed at Lund and refined in collaboration with Heinzl at Munich. Distinct from Sweet-style multi-position deflection: Hertz uses charge to either let drops pass to substrate (uncharged) or deflect them all to gutter (charged), with grayscale produced by varying the number of drops per pixel column. This is the architectural primitive behind Iris/Stork high-resolution continuous-inkjet proofers and the foundation for the entire 'Hertz CIJ' branch of the technology. Anticipates: continuous-inkjet systems using binary on-off deflection and drop-count-modulated grayscale, distinct from charge-amplitude-modulated multi-position deflection. The Heinzl-Hertz comprehensive 1985 review is also foundational prior art for the field. Important for distinguishing Hertz CIJ from Sweet CIJ in priority disputes.
