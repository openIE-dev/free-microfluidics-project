## Endo / Saito 1977 Canon Bubble Jet Priority Disclosure (1977)

- **id**: `endo-saito-1977-canon-bubble-jet-priority`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: inkjet-printhead
- **creator**: Ichiro Endo and Yasushi Saito (Canon Inc.)
- **disclosure**: Ichiro Endo and Yasushi Saito, Canon internal disclosure 1977 (the 'fortuitous bubble' incident); Canon JP-S54-59936 patent application filed 1977; corresponding US Patent 4,723,129 (1988) and the Canon Bubble Jet announcement 1981
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Extension to wave 1 endo-1979-canon-thermal-bubble-jet-foundation entry, sharpened to the actual 1977 priority moment. Canon's official corporate history places the discovery at 1977 when Endo accidentally observed ink ejection from a syringe touched by a hot soldering iron. The JP filing followed quickly. This priority date is contemporaneous with Stemme (1979) and predates HP Vaught/Cloutier (1981), making the priority dispute among the three a foundational element of inkjet history. Anticipates: thermal-bubble-jet ejection mechanism in the Canon priority lineage. Architectural primitive: (thin-film resistor + ink chamber + orifice + transient bubble drives drop ejection) - identical mechanism to Stemme but with independent priority. Useful for any patent dispute involving thermal-bubble-jet priority arguments.

## Stemme 1979 Thermal Inkjet Priority Patent (Sweden) (1979)

- **id**: `stemme-1979-thermal-inkjet-priority-patent`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: inkjet-printhead
- **creator**: Eric L. Stemme (independent inventor, Sweden)
- **disclosure**: Eric L. Stemme, Swedish patent SE7902783 (1979); subsequent US Patent 4,238,807 (1980) - 'Apparatus for inducing controlled expansion of vapor or gas bubbles within a liquid'
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Critical historical anchor: Stemme's 1979 Swedish patent disclosed the thermal-bubble-jet ejection principle BEFORE both HP (Vaught/Cloutier 1981) and Canon (Endo/Saito 1979 internal disclosure). The principle is the formation of a vapor bubble by rapid resistive heating of ink at a wall, with bubble expansion ejecting a drop through the adjacent orifice. Anticipates: any patent claiming thermal-resistor-induced bubble-jet drop ejection from a fluid chamber. The architectural primitive is (resistive heater + ink-filled chamber + orifice + bubble-driven drop ejection) as the foundational thermal inkjet mechanism. Both HP and Canon's later patents must be evaluated against Stemme's priority. Major prior art whose existence is poorly recognized in the inkjet patent literature.
