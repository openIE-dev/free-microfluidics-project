## JAXA Hayabusa-2 Sample Return Capsule (Ryugu) (2018)

- **id**: `jaxa-hayabusa-2-sample-return-capsule`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) / ISAS
- **disclosure**: Tachibana, S. et al. 'Pebbles and sand on asteroid (162173) Ryugu: in situ observation and particles returned to Earth.' Science 375, 1011-1016 (2022), doi:10.1126/science.abj8624; Sawada, H. et al. 'Hayabusa2 Sampler: Collection of Asteroidal Surface Material.' Space Science Reviews 208, 81-106 (2017); capsule return 2020-12-06
- **ip status**: unknown
- **prior art notes**: Discloses an upgrade to the Hayabusa-1 capsule architecture introducing: (a) gas-tight sample container (vacuum integrity better than 1 Pa over months) using indium-coated knife-edge metal seal qualified for cryogenic regolith volatiles; (b) three-chamber catcher partitioned to preserve TD1, TD2, and SCI ejecta provenance; (c) projectile-deflector internal flow path that decelerates 300-m/s projectile-launched particles into the catcher without thermal decomposition; (d) integration with a downstream gas-extraction manifold built into the curation facility for direct mass-spec measurement of trapped headspace prior to opening the capsule for solid-phase extraction. Anticipates: (a) gas-tight asteroid-sample curation manifolds; (b) projectile-impact micro-sampling fluid-handling architectures; (c) hand-off from sample-return capsule to vacuum-cleanroom microfluidic preparation lines (now standard for Bennu/OSIRIS-REx). Cite against any patent claiming a vacuum-sealed planetary-sample chamber with built-in gas-extraction port.
