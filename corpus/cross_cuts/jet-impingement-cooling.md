## DARPA ICECool Embedded Two-Phase Cooling for High-Power Microelectronics (2012-09)

- **id**: `darpa-icecool`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: cooling-substrate
- **creator**: DARPA Microsystems Technology Office + multiple academic and industry teams (Georgia Tech / Stanford / Lockheed / Raytheon)
- **disclosure**: Bar-Cohen, ITherm 2014 keynote; DARPA-BAA-12-50 ICECool Applications BAA 2012-09; Drummond et al., IEEE Trans. Compon. Packag. Manuf. Technol. 2018, 8, 1879-1890.
- **ip status**: open-permissive
- **prior art notes**: DARPA ICECool program funded multiple academic and industry teams to develop intra-chip embedded two-phase microchannel cooling for next-gen high-power electronics. Discloses (across the program output): DRIE-fabricated microchannels in chip back side; jet-impingement geometries for localized hot-spot cooling; two-phase refrigerant cooling loops integrated into chip packages. Anticipates: monolithically-integrated intra-chip microchannel cooling co-fabricated with the active device; two-phase boiling cooling as the path to >1 kW/cm^2 thermal management; the architectural pattern of cooling-fluid-as-part-of-the-chip-package vs cooling-as-an-external-cold-plate. Defensively important against patent claims to integrated microchannel cooling for radar and HPC.
