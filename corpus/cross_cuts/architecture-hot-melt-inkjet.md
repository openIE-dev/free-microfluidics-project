## Exxon Office Systems Qwip Hot-Melt Inkjet (historical) (1980)

- **id**: `exxon-office-systems-hot-melt-inkjet-historical`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: inkjet-printhead
- **creator**: Exxon Office Systems Corporation
- **disclosure**: Exxon Office Systems Qwip product literature circa 1980; US Patent 4,490,728 (Vaught/Exxon, related work) and Exxon hot-melt inkjet patents early 1980s; corporate history of Exxon's brief office-equipment foray (divested mid-1980s)
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Historical anchor for Exxon's brief 1980-1985 attempt to enter office equipment, which included pioneering hot-melt inkjet work that predated Howtek and Tektronix. Exxon Office Systems was divested in the mid-1980s but its inkjet patents and engineers seeded subsequent commercial efforts (some going to Diconix, others to Spectra/Dataproducts). Architectural primitives are similar to Howtek but Exxon's predates by ~4 years. Anticipates: very early hot-melt phase-change inkjet for office applications, important for establishing the priority date of the entire hot-melt inkjet category. Marked draft due to limited primary source availability for products that shipped in very small quantities.

## Howtek Pixelmaster Hot-Melt Inkjet Printer (historical) (1984)

- **id**: `howtek-pixelmaster-hot-melt-inkjet-historical`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: inkjet-printhead
- **creator**: Howtek Inc.
- **disclosure**: Howtek Inc. Pixelmaster product launch 1984 (Hudson, NH); Howtek hot-melt phase-change inkjet patent US4751517 (1988); 'Inkjet Printing of Hot Melt Inks' Howtek technical disclosure 1985
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Historical foundational entry for hot-melt phase-change inkjet, a category later dominated by Tektronix Phaser/Xerox ColorQube. Architectural primitive: the printhead is heated and continuously maintains the ink in molten state; jetted drops solidify on substrate contact via phase change rather than evaporation or absorption. This eliminates the bleed/dot-gain problems of liquid inkjet on uncoated paper and the dry-time requirements of solvent inkjet. Anticipates: phase-change inkjet printers using heated piezo printheads with wax-based ink solidifying on substrate contact. Foundational prior art for the entire hot-melt inkjet category, predating the better-known Tektronix Phaser by several years.
