## Hybridoma technology for monoclonal antibody production (1975)

- **id**: `kohler-milstein-1975-hybridoma`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Georges Kohler, Cesar Milstein (MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology, Cambridge)
- **disclosure**: Kohler, G.; Milstein, C. Continuous cultures of fused cells secreting antibody of predefined specificity. Nature 1975, 256, 495-497. DOI: 10.1038/256495a0
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: The source of essentially every monoclonal capture and detection antibody used in microfluidic immunoassays, lateral-flow strips, and bead-based multiplex panels. Kohler and Milstein famously did not patent the method (a decision later criticized in the UK). Anticipates generic claims to monoclonal-antibody-based affinity reagents and to clonal cell-line antibody manufacture. Any microfluidic diagnostic claiming novelty in its use of monoclonal antibody pairs as the recognition element contends with this disclosure as the enabling prior art for the reagent itself.

## Centro de Inmunologia Molecular monoclonal-antibody process platform (1994)

- **id**: `centro-inmunologia-molecular-mab-process-platform`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Centro de Inmunologia Molecular (CIM), Havana
- **disclosure**: Lage, A.; et al. Center of Molecular Immunology (CIM) publications on nimotuzumab (h-R3) and CIMAvax-EGF; CIM bioprocess descriptions, Havana, from circa 1994
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Process-side entry. The CIM produces therapeutic monoclonal antibodies (the recognition reagents used in immunoassay cartridges worldwide) via perfusion bioreactors, with microvolume immunoassay QC. Relevant prior art for monoclonal-antibody manufacture and for integrated bioprocess-monitoring microfluidic analytics. Reagent-supply lineage from Kohler-Milstein hybridoma technology.
