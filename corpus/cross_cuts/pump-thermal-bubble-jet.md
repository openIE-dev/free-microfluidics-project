## Canon thermal Bubble Jet inkjet printhead (1979)

- **id**: `canon-bubble-jet-printhead`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: inkjet-printhead
- **creator**: Canon Inc., Endo et al.
- **disclosure**: Endo, I.; Sato, Y.; Saito, S.; Nakagiri, T.; Ohno, S. Liquid jet recording process and apparatus therefor. JP S54-59936A (1979); subsequent US patents include US4723129 (1988).
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Foundational disclosure of thermal bubble-jet inkjet printheads — almost certainly the highest-volume microfluidic device ever produced. Anticipates: integrated silicon-based microfluidic channels with embedded thin-film resistive heaters, on-demand vapor-bubble droplet ejection from microscale orifices, and the entire thermal-inkjet category of consumer printers. Microfluidics' largest commercial deployment by orders of magnitude predates the academic field's emergence.

## Thermal bubble-jet foundation (Endo / Canon, 1979) (1979)

- **id**: `endo-1979-canon-thermal-bubble-jet-foundation`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: inkjet-printhead
- **creator**: Endo et al. (Canon)
- **disclosure**: Endo, I.; Sato, Y.; Saito, S.; Nakagiri, T.; Ohno, S. (Canon) Liquid jet recording process and apparatus therefor. JP Patent 54-161935 (1979); equivalent US4429321A.
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Foundational disclosure (more detailed than the Canon-1979 Bubble Jet entry) of the silicon-MEMS thermal-bubble-jet printhead with electrically pulsed thin-film resistive heater, vapor-bubble-driven droplet ejection through an integrated nozzle. The original Canon team's accidental discovery: a soldering iron contacted an ink syringe, ejecting a droplet — leading to the entire thermal-inkjet category. Anticipates everything in the thermal-inkjet lineage from HP ThinkJet through Memjet.

## HP ThinkJet thermal inkjet printhead (1984)

- **id**: `hp-thinkjet-thermal-inkjet`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: inkjet-printhead
- **creator**: Hewlett-Packard, John Vaught et al.
- **disclosure**: Hewlett-Packard ThinkJet (HP 2225), launched 1984; thermal-inkjet patent family includes US4490728 (Vaught & Hackleman, 1984).
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: HP's parallel disclosure of thermal-inkjet printing, launched commercially in the ThinkJet (HP 2225) in 1984. Independent of Canon's bubble-jet line; together they establish thermal-inkjet as a category. Anticipates: thermally pulsed liquid ejection from silicon-microfabricated chambers, replaceable printhead cartridges, and the consumable-cartridge business model that mass-microfluidics enabled.

## Memjet thermal silicon-MEMS printhead (2007)

- **id**: `memjet-printhead`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: inkjet-printhead
- **creator**: Memjet (Silverbrook Research)
- **disclosure**: Memjet (Silverbrook Research) printhead architecture. https://memjet.com
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: High-density thermal-bubble-jet printhead with ~70,000 nozzles per page-wide module on a single silicon MEMS die. Anticipates: page-wide-array thermal-inkjet architecture, sub-200 µm nozzle pitch on silicon, and integrated drive electronics + nozzle-plate co-fabrication.
