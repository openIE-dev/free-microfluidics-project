## Quantitative organic microanalysis (1911)

- **id**: `pregl-1911-quantitative-organic-microanalysis`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Fritz Pregl (University of Graz / University of Innsbruck)
- **disclosure**: Pregl, F. Die quantitative organische Mikroanalyse. Springer, Berlin, 1917 (methods developed and presented from 1911; Nobel Prize 1923)
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: The methodological root of microscale quantitative analysis. By cutting sample requirements by roughly two orders of magnitude and developing the microbalance and micro-combustion apparatus, Pregl established the premise that underlies all of microfluidics: that complete quantitative analysis can be performed on vanishingly small samples. While not microfluidic in the channel sense, it is the conceptual ancestor cited when arguing that miniaturized total analysis is an incremental scaling of a century-old program rather than a novel paradigm. Relevant background prior art for claims framing micro-volume quantitative analysis as inherently novel.
