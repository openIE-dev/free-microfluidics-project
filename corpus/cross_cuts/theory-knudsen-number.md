## Knudsen 1909 — The molecular current of gases through tubes (Knudsen number) (1909)

- **id**: `knudsen-1909-gas-flow-narrow-tubes`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Martin Knudsen
- **disclosure**: M. Knudsen, 'Die Gesetze der Molekularströmung und der inneren Reibungsströmung der Gase durch Röhren', Annalen der Physik 333(1), 75-130 (1909)
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: Foundational treatment of gas flow in narrow tubes when mean free path is comparable to channel width; introduces what becomes known as the Knudsen number Kn=λ/L and the slip/transition/free-molecular flow regimes. Anticipates: any nanofluidic or vacuum-microfluidic patent that claims operation in the slip or transition regime as novel — the regime classification is 117 years old. Particularly load-bearing for: Knudsen-pump devices, MEMS gas sensors, and nanopore gas-transport devices.

## Karniadakis, Beskok & Aluru 2005 — Microflows and Nanoflows: Fundamentals and Simulation (textbook) (2005-08-10)

- **id**: `karniadakis-beskok-aluru-2005-microflows-nanoflows-textbook`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: George Em Karniadakis, Ali Beskok, Narayana R. Aluru
- **disclosure**: ISBN 978-0387221977; Springer Interdisciplinary Applied Mathematics vol 29, 2005
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: Definitive treatment of gas and liquid flows from continuum to molecular regime, with emphasis on slip flow, Knudsen-number scaling, and DSMC/MD simulation methods for nanoscale transport. Anticipates: patents on gas-flow microsensors, slip-flow channels, Knudsen-pump devices, and molecular-scale nanofluidic transport — all reduce to textbook physics. Particularly load-bearing for nanofluidic-channel patents claiming non-continuum effects as novel.
