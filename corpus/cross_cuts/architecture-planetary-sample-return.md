## Stardust Aerogel Comet/Interstellar Dust Sample Collector (1999)

- **id**: `stardust-aerogel-comet-sample-collector`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: NASA / JPL / Lockheed Martin / University of Washington (PI: Donald Brownlee)
- **disclosure**: Tsou, P. et al. 'Wild 2 and Interstellar Sample Collection and Earth Return.' Journal of Geophysical Research 108 (E10), 8113 (2003), doi:10.1029/2003JE002109; Brownlee, D. E. et al. 'Comet 81P/Wild 2 Under a Microscope.' Science 314, 1711-1716 (2006); spacecraft launch 1999-02-07, sample return 2006-01-15
- **ip status**: unknown
- **prior art notes**: Discloses (a) the engineering of low-density (5-50 mg/cm^3) silica aerogel for use as a hypervelocity-particle capture medium in deep space; (b) a two-sided indexed collector tray that distinguishes cometary (B-side) from interstellar (A-side) particles; (c) a curation workflow in which aerogel volumes containing single tracks are excised as 'keystones,' mounted on silicon picoforks, and serially analyzed by synchrotron and ion-beam techniques. Although not a fluidic device per se, the keystone-extraction-to-analysis chain incorporates microfluidic acid-extraction protocols (e.g., HF dissolution of aerogel in PTFE microreactors for residual particle recovery) that have become canonical in cosmochemistry, and the aerogel itself functions as a porous capture matrix relevant to astrobiology aerosol-sampling concepts (e.g., Enceladus/ELSAH plume capture). Cite against any later patent claiming aerogel-based hypervelocity capture trays with side-indexed dual collection.

## JAXA Hayabusa-1 Sample Return Capsule (Itokawa) (2006)

- **id**: `jaxa-hayabusa-1-sample-return-capsule`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) / ISAS
- **disclosure**: Yano, H. et al. 'Touchdown of the Hayabusa Spacecraft at the Muses Sea on Itokawa.' Science 312, 1350-1353 (2006), doi:10.1126/science.1126164; Abe, M. et al. 'Hayabusa Sample Container.' Lunar and Planetary Science Conference XLI (2010), abstract 1638; sample return 2010-06-13
- **ip status**: unknown
- **prior art notes**: Discloses a sample-return capsule integrating an asteroid sample-handling subsystem comprising a horn-shaped sampler, an electrically actuated sample-transfer chamber, and a hermetically sealed catcher canister that is opened only inside an N2-purged curation cleanroom. Although bulk-flow rather than micro-flow, the curation handling chain - including FFP-class cleanroom integration with electrostatic micromanipulation of micrometer-scale particles into glass nanofluidic capillaries - constitutes prior art for downstream microfluidic asteroid sample analysis. Anticipates: (a) hermetic hot-weld lid mechanisms for sample-return capsules; (b) dual-chamber sample partitioning to preserve provenance per touchdown; (c) the curation-to-microfluidics handoff pattern (electrostatic pickup, glass capillary mounting, ion-beam cross-section) used for FIB-tomography and synchrotron analysis of returned grains. Cite against any later capsule patent claiming dual-chamber pyro-sealed asteroid-grain catchers.

## JAXA Hayabusa-2 Sample Return Capsule (Ryugu) (2018)

- **id**: `jaxa-hayabusa-2-sample-return-capsule`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) / ISAS
- **disclosure**: Tachibana, S. et al. 'Pebbles and sand on asteroid (162173) Ryugu: in situ observation and particles returned to Earth.' Science 375, 1011-1016 (2022), doi:10.1126/science.abj8624; Sawada, H. et al. 'Hayabusa2 Sampler: Collection of Asteroidal Surface Material.' Space Science Reviews 208, 81-106 (2017); capsule return 2020-12-06
- **ip status**: unknown
- **prior art notes**: Discloses an upgrade to the Hayabusa-1 capsule architecture introducing: (a) gas-tight sample container (vacuum integrity better than 1 Pa over months) using indium-coated knife-edge metal seal qualified for cryogenic regolith volatiles; (b) three-chamber catcher partitioned to preserve TD1, TD2, and SCI ejecta provenance; (c) projectile-deflector internal flow path that decelerates 300-m/s projectile-launched particles into the catcher without thermal decomposition; (d) integration with a downstream gas-extraction manifold built into the curation facility for direct mass-spec measurement of trapped headspace prior to opening the capsule for solid-phase extraction. Anticipates: (a) gas-tight asteroid-sample curation manifolds; (b) projectile-impact micro-sampling fluid-handling architectures; (c) hand-off from sample-return capsule to vacuum-cleanroom microfluidic preparation lines (now standard for Bennu/OSIRIS-REx). Cite against any patent claiming a vacuum-sealed planetary-sample chamber with built-in gas-extraction port.
