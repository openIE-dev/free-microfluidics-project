## UCL Goyanes Group Printlets (3D-Printed Personalized Tablets) (2014)

- **id**: `ucl-goyanes-printlets-3d-tablets`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: lab-on-chip
- **creator**: University College London (PI: Abdul Basit, Alvaro Goyanes, Simon Gaisford)
- **disclosure**: Goyanes, A.; Buanz, A. B. M.; Basit, A. W.; Gaisford, S. 'Fused-filament 3D printing (3DP) for fabrication of tablets.' International Journal of Pharmaceutics 476, 88-92 (2014), doi:10.1016/j.ijpharm.2014.09.044; Goyanes, A. et al. 'Automated therapy preparation of isoleucine formulations using 3D printing for the treatment of MSUD: First single-centre, prospective, crossover study in patients.' International Journal of Pharmaceutics 567, 118497 (2019)
- **ip status**: open-permissive
- **prior art notes**: Discloses the UCL/FabRx body of academic literature establishing FDM 3D printing as a feasible route for pharmaceutical solid oral dosage forms. Anticipates: (a) the use of geometric design (shape, infill density, surface area) as a primary lever to control drug-dissolution kinetics independent of formulation; (b) multi-active 'polypill' tablets manufactured in a single 3D print job; (c) clinical translation pathway for personalized 3D-printed tablets in pediatric inborn-errors-of-metabolism patients. Cite against later patents claiming geometric-control-of-dissolution 3D-printed pharmaceutical tablets.

## Aprecia Spritam (3D-Printed Levetiracetam) (2015)

- **id**: `aprecia-spritam-3d-printed-levetiracetam`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: lab-on-chip
- **creator**: Aprecia Pharmaceuticals
- **disclosure**: FDA Approval, Spritam (levetiracetam) Tablet for Oral Suspension, NDA 207958, approved 2015-08-03 (first FDA-approved 3D-printed drug); Yu, D. G. et al. '3D printed flexible drug delivery system based on rapidly disintegrating tablets fabricated by ZipDose technology.' International Journal of Pharmaceutics 540, 90-97 (2018), doi:10.1016/j.ijpharm.2018.01.012
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Discloses the Aprecia ZipDose technology - the first FDA-approved 3D-printed drug. The technology combines: (a) MIT-licensed binder-jetting 3D-printing in which an aqueous binder is jetted from a thermal-inkjet-class printhead onto a powder bed of API + excipients; (b) layer-by-layer construction of a porous high-dose tablet (up to 1000 mg API in a 17 mm tablet); (c) post-processing freeze-drying and packaging compatible with rapid-disintegration tablet handling. Anticipates: (a) binder-jetting 3D printing as a regulator-validated GMP manufacturing route for solid oral dosage forms; (b) the architectural pattern of using ink-jet printheads as the dosing-and-shaping element in pharma manufacturing; (c) high-dose oral-disintegrating tablets manufactured by 3D printing. Cite against later patents claiming binder-jet 3D-printed pharmaceutical tablets.

## Triastek MED (Melt Extrusion Deposition) 3D-Printing Platform (2020)

- **id**: `triastek-med-3d-printing-platform`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: printer-tooling
- **creator**: Triastek Inc. (Nanjing, China)
- **disclosure**: Triastek Inc. press releases 2020-2023 (T19, T20, T21, T22 IND filings); FDA IND clearance T19 (rivaroxaban 3D-printed) 2020-12; Goyanes, A. et al. 'Patient acceptability of 3D printed medicines.' International Journal of Pharmaceutics 530, 71-78 (2017), doi:10.1016/j.ijpharm.2017.07.064
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Discloses Triastek's MED platform combining hot-melt extrusion (HME) with FDM 3D printing in a continuous in-line process. The HME barrel produces API+polymer melt that is directly extruded through a printhead nozzle (no intermediate filament), depositing layered tablet geometries with engineered drug-release compartments. Anticipates: (a) direct HME-to-FDM continuous printing as an alternative to filament-based pharmaceutical FDM; (b) multi-compartment tablet geometries with spatially programmed drug-release kinetics; (c) the regulatory template for IND clearance of 3D-printed reformulations of approved APIs. Cite against later patents claiming direct-extrusion HME-FDM pharmaceutical 3D printers.

## AM4Pharma Semisolid 3D Pharmaceutical Printer (2020)

- **id**: `am4pharma-semisolid-3d-printer`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: printer-tooling
- **creator**: AM4Pharma Oy (University of Helsinki spinout)
- **disclosure**: Khatri, P.; Shah, M. K.; Vora, N. 'Formulation strategies for solid oral dosage form using 3D printing technology.' Journal of Drug Delivery Science and Technology 46, 148-155 (2018), doi:10.1016/j.jddst.2018.05.009; AM4Pharma Oy company technical brochure (Univ Helsinki spinout) 2020
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Discloses the AM4Pharma semisolid extrusion 3D pharmaceutical printer for heat-sensitive APIs. Unlike FDM-based printers, the SSE platform extrudes pre-mixed pharmaceutical pastes at room temperature, enabling printing of biologics, peptides, and proteins that would denature in HME. Anticipates: (a) room-temperature SSE 3D printers as a heat-sensitive-API alternative to FDM/HME pharmaceutical printing; (b) hydrogel-paste API formulations as printable feedstocks for SSE; (c) the small-batch personalized-medicine business model targeting orphan and rare diseases. Cite against later patents claiming room-temperature SSE pharmaceutical 3D printers.
