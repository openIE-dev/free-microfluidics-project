## The motion and precipitation of suspensoids in divergent electric fields (dielectrophoresis foundation) (1951)

- **id**: `pohl-1951-dielectrophoresis-foundation`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: H. A. Pohl
- **disclosure**: Pohl, H. A. The motion and precipitation of suspensoids in divergent electric fields. J. Appl. Phys. 1951, 22, 869–871. DOI: 10.1063/1.1700065
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: Foundational disclosure of dielectrophoresis (DEP): the motion of polarizable particles in non-uniform electric fields, distinct from electrophoresis (which moves charged particles in uniform fields). Predates microfluidics by 40 years. Anticipates: the entire DEP-on-chip subfield (Gascoyne, Voldman, Pethig). Critical because much later DEP-on-chip disclosure can be reduced in scope by reaching back to Pohl for the foundational physics.

## Hughes & Markx DEP Textbook (Nanoelectromechanics in Engineering and Biology) (1999)

- **id**: `hughes-markx-2002-dep-textbook-foundation`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Michael Hughes (Surrey) and Gerard Markx (Heriot-Watt)
- **disclosure**: Markx G. H., Davey C. L., Enzyme and Microbial Technology 25:161 (1999), doi:10.1016/S0141-0229(99)00048-2; Hughes M. P., Nanoelectromechanics in Engineering and Biology, CRC Press 2002, ISBN 9780849311833
- **ip status**: open-permissive
- **prior art notes**: Hughes 2002 textbook and Markx 1999 review codify the field of dielectrophoresis (DEP) for biological microfluidics, providing element-by-element disclosure of: (a) electrode geometries (interdigitated, polynomial, castellated, parallel-bar); (b) DEP modalities (positive/negative DEP, traveling-wave DEP, electrorotation, DEP-FFF); (c) integration with microfluidic flow channels; (d) cell-electric-properties database. Anticipates broad downstream patent claims combining DEP electrode geometries with microfluidic flow for cell separation, trapping, and characterization. Foundational complement to pohl-1951-dielectrophoresis-foundation, gascoyne-2002-dep-cancer-cells, and voldman-2002-cell-trap-dep-array.

## Dielectrophoresis-based separation of human cancer cells from blood (2002)

- **id**: `gascoyne-2002-dep-cancer-cells`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: separator-component
- **creator**: Gascoyne group, MD Anderson
- **disclosure**: Gascoyne, P. R. C.; Vykoukal, J. Particle separation by dielectrophoresis. Electrophoresis 2002, 23, 1973–1983. DOI: 10.1002/1522-2683(200207)23:13<1973::AID-ELPS1973>3.0.CO;2-1
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Established practical DEP-FFF (dielectrophoretic field-flow fractionation) for cell separation, particularly CTC enrichment from blood. Demonstrates positive-DEP capture of cancer cells against negative-DEP blood cells using castellated electrode arrays. Anticipates: castellated-electrode-array DEP architecture, DEP-FFF as a continuous-flow separation method, and the commercial DEPArray and ApoCell platforms.

## Microfabricated dielectrophoretic single-cell trap arrays (2002)

- **id**: `voldman-2002-cell-trap-dep-array`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: single-cell-platform
- **creator**: Voldman, Toner, Schmidt (MIT / Harvard)
- **disclosure**: Voldman, J.; Gray, M. L.; Toner, M.; Schmidt, M. A. A microfabrication-based dynamic array cytometer. Anal. Chem. 2002, 74, 3984–3990. DOI: 10.1021/ac0256235

## Manaresi 2003 CMOS DEP Cage Array (DEPArray foundation) (2003-12)

- **id**: `manaresi-2003-deparray-cmos-foundation`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: single-cell-platform
- **creator**: Manaresi, Romani, Medoro, Tartagni, Guerrieri (U Bologna / Silicon Biosystems)
- **disclosure**: Manaresi N., Romani A., Medoro G., Altomare L., Leonardi A., Tartagni M., Guerrieri R., IEEE Journal of Solid-State Circuits 38(12):2297-2305 (2003), doi:10.1109/JSSC.2003.819171
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Foundational JSSC paper disclosing a CMOS chip with a 320x320 array of individually addressable electrodes capable of generating, translating, and merging closed DEP potential cages around individual cells. Direct ancestor of menarini-deparray and menarini-silicon-biosystems-deparray-nxt.

## Chiou & Wu Optoelectronic Tweezer (OET) Foundation (2005-07)

- **id**: `chiou-wu-2005-optoelectronic-tweezer-foundation`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: single-cell-platform
- **creator**: Pei Yu Chiou, Aaron Ohta, Ming C. Wu (UC Berkeley)
- **disclosure**: Chiou P. Y., Ohta A. T., Wu M. C., Nature 436:370-372 (2005), doi:10.1038/nature03831
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: The Nature 2005 paper discloses the foundational architecture of a photoconductive substrate (a-Si:H) sandwiched between transparent electrodes; projected light creates local conductivity that defines virtual DEP electrodes capable of trapping and translating cells. Anticipates: (i) all subsequent optoelectronic tweezer and OEP-class platforms (Berkeley Lights, etc.); (ii) the use of DMD-projected patterns to dynamically reconfigure DEP electrode geometry; (iii) hybrid optical-DEP particle manipulation. This is the academic ancestor of berkeley-lights-beacon-optofluidic.

## ApoCell ApoStream CTC Capture System (2012)

- **id**: `apocell-apostream-ctc-dep`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: single-cell-platform
- **creator**: ApoCell Inc. (acquired by Precision for Medicine)
- **disclosure**: Gupta V., Jafferji I., Garza M., et al., Biomicrofluidics 6:024133 (2012), doi:10.1063/1.4731647; ApoCell Inc. product literature; Precision for Medicine ApoStream technical brief 2018
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: ApoStream discloses a continuous-flow DEP field-flow fractionation chamber wherein a PCB interdigitated electrode array generates a non-uniform AC field that levitates erythrocytes (negative DEP) while CTCs (different polarizability) flow at a different streamline elevation, enabling label-free separation. Anticipates: (i) continuous-flow DEP-FFF CTC capture systems; (ii) PCB-electrode microfluidic devices for blood processing; (iii) antibody-independent rare-cell isolation by polarizability. Lineage descends from Gascoyne 2002 DEP cancer-cell foundation but adds productized cartridge and multi-mL/hr throughput.

## Berkeley Lights Beacon (PhenomeX/Bruker) (2016-09)

- **id**: `berkeley-lights-beacon-optofluidic`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: single-cell-platform
- **creator**: Berkeley Lights Inc. (later PhenomeX, acquired by Bruker 2023)
- **disclosure**: Berkeley Lights Inc. Beacon launch press release Sep 2016; Le K. et al. SLAS Discovery 23(1):44-55 (2018), doi:10.1177/2472555217735282; OptoSelect chip US patent 9,403,172
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Berkeley Lights Beacon discloses an optoelectronic positioning (OEP) chip wherein a virtual electrode pattern is generated by projecting light onto a photoconductive layer (a-Si:H) integrated into a microfluidic chamber; the resulting localized DEP forces translate single cells into NanoPen sequestration pens for clonal expansion and serial assay.
