## Sweet 1965 Continuous-Inkjet Deflection Patent (extended) (1965)

- **id**: `sweet-1965-deflection-patent-extended`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: inkjet-printhead
- **creator**: Richard G. Sweet (Stanford University)
- **disclosure**: Richard G. Sweet, 'High Frequency Recording with Electrostatically Deflected Ink Jets', Review of Scientific Instruments 36:131-136 (1965), doi:10.1063/1.1719502; US Patent 3,373,437 (filed 1964, granted 1968) 'Fluid droplet recorder with a plurality of jets'
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: Extended foundational entry for Sweet's continuous-inkjet deflection mechanism. The 1965 RSI paper and the 1968 US3373437 patent jointly disclose the architectural primitive of (piezo-stimulated jet + Rayleigh breakup + selective charging via charge tunnel + parallel-plate electrostatic deflection + gutter for uncharged drops + ink recirculation), which is the foundation of every subsequent continuous-inkjet system from IBM 6640 to modern Hitachi/Linx/Domino industrial coders. Anticipates: continuous-inkjet recording, marking, and addressing systems using charge-coded electrostatic deflection. Foundational prior art for the entire CIJ category. Distinct from wave-1 sweet-1965-electrostatic-modulation-cij entry (which focuses on the modulation aspect); this entry isolates the deflection mechanism specifically.
