## Huygens Aerosol Collector and Pyrolyser (ACP) (2002)

- **id**: `cassini-huygens-acp-aerosol-collector-pyrolyser`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: lab-on-chip
- **creator**: Service d'Aeronomie / CNRS / ESA / NASA (PI: Guy Israel, Service d'Aeronomie)
- **disclosure**: Israel, G. et al. 'Huygens Probe Aerosol Collector Pyrolyser Experiment.' Space Science Reviews 104, 433-468 (2002), doi:10.1023/A:1023688604877; ESA Huygens probe payload documentation 1997-2005 (mission landing 2005-01-14)
- **ip status**: unknown
- **prior art notes**: Discloses a planetary-probe pyrolysis sample-handling subsystem in which a porous-metal filter cartridge is exposed to atmospheric flow at two distinct altitude windows during parachute descent on Titan, then sealed and indexed into a three-stage furnace where helium sweep gas at controlled overpressure carries thermally evolved species through a heated transfer capillary into a downstream GCMS. Anticipates: (a) the architectural pattern of in-situ aerosol pyrolysis as a sample-prep front-end to a planetary GCMS; (b) two-altitude differential aerosol collection as a stratification technique for atmospheric chemistry; (c) the rotary cartridge indexer common to later Mars MOMA, SAM, and Dragonfly DraMS oven sample stations; (d) helium-sweep evolved-gas transfer with stepwise temperature ramping as a primary means of separating volatile, semi-volatile, and refractory tholin components in the same sample; (e) survival/qualification of the pyrolysis sample-handling architecture under entry-loads and Titan thermal regime (94 K). Should be cited against any later patent claiming planetary aerosol-pyrolysis sample-prep with multi-altitude collection or rotary furnace indexing into a downstream MS.

## SOLID (Signs Of Life Detector) Antibody Microarray Platform (2008)

- **id**: `nasa-solid-signs-of-life-detector`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: lab-on-chip
- **creator**: Centro de Astrobiologia (CAB-INTA-CSIC), Madrid (PI: Victor Parro)
- **disclosure**: Parro, V. et al. 'SOLID2: An Antibody Array-Based Life-Detector Instrument in a Mars Drilling Simulation Experiment.' Astrobiology 8(5), 987-999 (2008), doi:10.1089/ast.2007.0211; Parro, V. et al. 'A Microbial Oasis in the Hypersaline Atacama Subsurface Discovered by a Life Detector Chip.' Astrobiology 11(10), 969-996 (2011), doi:10.1089/ast.2011.0654
- **ip status**: unknown
- **prior art notes**: Discloses an in-situ life-detection instrument architecture combining (i) an automated Sample Preparation Unit that delivers a slurry of crushed regolith to a filtration/extraction module under ultrasonic agitation, (ii) a multi-channel antibody microarray (LDChip) printed on functionalized glass with hundreds of polyclonal antibodies covering proteins, lipopolysaccharides, exopolysaccharides, and intact cells, and (iii) a fluorescence sandwich-immunoassay readout. Anticipates: (a) end-to-end planetary microfluidic immunoassay platforms (in particular for ExoMars and ELSAH-class missions); (b) ultrasonic in-cartridge extraction as a sample-prep front-end for regolith biosignature analysis; (c) multiplex polyclonal-antibody arrays as the molecular-recognition layer of a life-detection chip rather than oligonucleotides or mass spec; (d) a rugged ground-tested fluidic cartridge architecture for autonomous astrobiology immunoassay validated in Mars-analog deep-drill (Rio Tinto IPBSL) campaigns. Cite against any later claim to fluidic immunoassay life-detection cartridges with regolith sonication front-ends.
