## Hagen 1839 — On the motion of water in narrow cylindrical tubes (Hagen-Poiseuille flow) (1839)

- **id**: `hagen-1839-pipe-flow-viscosity`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Gotthilf Heinrich Ludwig Hagen
- **disclosure**: G. Hagen, 'Über die Bewegung des Wassers in engen cylindrischen Röhren', Poggendorffs Annalen der Physik und Chemie 46, 423-442 (1839)
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: First quantitative experimental description of laminar pressure-driven flow in narrow cylindrical tubes, predating Poiseuille's 1840 publication. Establishes the fourth-power dependence of volumetric flow on tube radius (Hagen-Poiseuille law). Foundational for ALL pressure-driven microfluidic theory: every channel-resistance calculation, every flow-controller specification, every droplet-generator design implicitly relies on this 1839 disclosure. Anticipates: any patent that recites 'flow rate determined by pressure drop and channel hydraulic resistance' as part of its operating principle — the relation is 187 years old.

## Poiseuille 1840 — Experimental research on the motion of liquids in tubes of very small diameter (1840)

- **id**: `poiseuille-1840-capillary-flow`
- **corpus**: academic
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Jean Léonard Marie Poiseuille
- **disclosure**: J.L.M. Poiseuille, 'Recherches expérimentales sur le mouvement des liquides dans les tubes de très petits diamètres', Comptes Rendus 11, 961-967 and 1041-1048 (1840); full memoir Mém. Savants Étrang. 9, 433-543 (1846)
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: Independent and more rigorous experimental establishment of pressure-driven laminar flow in capillary tubes; Poiseuille's name attaches to the law (Hagen-Poiseuille). For microfluidics, Poiseuille is the canonical 19th-century experimentalist demonstrating that the same physical relation governs flow in tubes from millimeter to capillary scale. Anticipates: any patent claim to a microfluidic 'channel of small diameter exhibiting laminar flow with parabolic velocity profile' as the operative physics; the parabolic profile in capillaries was experimentally established 1840.
