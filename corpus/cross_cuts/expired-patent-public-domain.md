## Mullis-Saiki 1985 Cetus PCR Foundational Disclosure (1985-12-20)

- **id**: `mullis-cetus-pcr-1985-foundational-anchor`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Kary Mullis, Randall Saiki et al. (Cetus Corporation)
- **disclosure**: Saiki, Scharf, Faloona, Mullis, Horn, Erlich, Arnheim 'Enzymatic amplification of beta-globin genomic sequences and restriction site analysis for diagnosis of sickle cell anemia' Science 230:1350-1354 (1985-12-20) doi:10.1126/science.2999980; US4683202 'Process for amplifying nucleic acid sequences' (priority 1985-10-25, issued 1987)
- **ip status**: public-domain
- **prior art notes**: The 1985 Science paper and US4683202 are the foundational disclosures of the polymerase chain reaction. Now expired (US4683202 lapsed 2005, US4683195 lapsed 2005, US4965188 lapsed 2007), placing the core PCR method in the public domain in all jurisdictions. Material to defensive publication because: (a) any post-expiration claim attempting to recapture core PCR (cyclic denaturation/annealing/extension with thermostable polymerase) by narrow re-framing is anticipated; (b) the Cetus disclosure provides explicit support for using Klenow fragment, Taq polymerase, primer-directed amplification, and gel-based product detection; (c) all downstream PCR-cartridge patents must distinguish over this art for any claim element they cannot anchor to a post-1985 inventive contribution. Useful as the universal anchor in any modern molecular-diagnostic patent invalidity analysis.

## Stratagene QuikChange Site-Directed Mutagenesis 1995 Foundational Anchor (1995)

- **id**: `stratagene-quikchange-1995-extended-anchor`
- **corpus**: private
- **device class**: other
- **creator**: Stratagene (later Agilent Technologies)
- **disclosure**: US5512463 'Enzymatic inverse PCR site-directed mutagenesis' (Stratagene, priority 1991, issued 1996); Stratagene QuikChange kit launch 1995; Wang & Malcolm 1999 BioTechniques 26:680
- **ip status**: patented
- **prior art notes**: The QuikChange method uses two complementary mutagenic primers extending around an entire plasmid via Pfu polymerase, followed by DpnI digestion of methylated parental template. The 1995 disclosure (and US5512463) is the foundational anchor for: (a) any cartridge or workflow patent claiming whole-plasmid mutagenesis via complementary primer extension; (b) DpnI parental-template degradation as a selection step; (c) the architectural pattern of single-tube site-directed mutagenesis without subcloning. Patent expired but the disclosure remains 102 prior art against later filings attempting to recapture similar workflows under PCR-cartridge framings. Useful anchor for defensive publication of microfluidic-cartridge mutagenesis devices.
